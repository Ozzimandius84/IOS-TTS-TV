## 1 · The rate the road has to hit — re-measured, and the second number

`node drops.mjs ~/…/TTSTV`. The 6 Sep table counted words a float never draws.
This one adds the number that table did not print: **how long the WRONG word
stands on the float** after the audio has moved past it. A road can score zero
drops and still be a bad float, because the eye is on it.

The corpus has grown a real alignment since 6 Sep — **five chapters, 1,739
words, are genuinely MMS_FA aligned**; the other 40 files are still the 350 ms
divider and flatter every road. The biggest real one is now a **whole chapter**,
not a fragment: `eclogues-en/c001`, 794 words, 263.3 s.

| chapter | words | 60 Hz | 30 Hz | 10 Hz | 4 Hz | 1 Hz |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| eclogues-en/c001 | 794 | **0** | **0** | 4 | 125 | 534 |
| poems/c002 | 578 | **0** | **0** | 3 | 87 | 344 |

| poems/c018 | 71 | **0** | **0** | 3 | 18 | 49 |
| poems/c021 | 75 | **0** | **0** | 0 | 5 | 44 |

*words never drawn at all.* And the staleness, on `eclogues-en/c001`:

| painter | late p95 | late max | **share of the word's own life spent showing the PREVIOUS word (p95)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 60 Hz | 15.7 ms | 16.3 ms | **9.9%** |
| 30 Hz | 31.7 ms | 33.0 ms | **20.6%** |
| 10 Hz | 95.0 ms | 99.0 ms | 58.8% |
| 4 Hz | 235 ms | 249 ms | 89.2% |
| 1 Hz | 667 ms | 999 ms | 96.7% |

Real-aligned gaps, n=1,734: **min 40 ms · p01 61 ms · p05 100 ms · median
281 ms → 3.56 words/s typical, 16.4/s at p01.**

**So 30 Hz is the floor and 60 Hz is the design point.** 30 Hz loses no word but
a fifth of every word's life is spent on the last one; at 60 Hz that is a tenth,
which is under the eye's own latency. Below 10 Hz it is not a word display at
all, and a `mediaSession`/Now-Playing write (1 Hz) shows 33% of the book.

## 2 · The chapter is the reader's own words, not a re-cut of the text

`build-timeline.mjs` lifts `tokenise` and `paraIndexOf` **verbatim out of
`reader/listen.js` at run time** and applies listen.js's own count check.
`eclogues-en/c001`: **101 paragraphs exact, 0 refused, 794 words.** A probe that
re-cut the chapter itself would be a second word index disagreeing with the
page's, and every drop count would be about a different book.

`node test-probe-c.cjs` — **16 passed, 0 failed**: the timeline is byte-for-byte
what listen.js would build; `probe-c/wordclock.js` is `scratch-float/`'s to the
byte, so probe-c and probe-b count the same word; `indexAt` is inclusive at a
word's start, −1 before the first, and the last word past the end.

## 3 · K26a — does the word keep up: **yes in the foreground, and the number is 794/794**

`float.html` driven in headless Chromium 141 in the Cowork container, the whole
chapter end to end, real time (265 s, no acceleration):

```
audio ran to  265.10 s  (word 793 of 794)
words the clock passed  794
words the float DREW    794
NEVER DRAWN             0   (0.0%)
counters {"raf":15906,"timer":16566,"tu":0,"worklet":15215,"paint":47687}
```

**0 of 794.** The painter is the audio clock, never `rAF`'s own count: `paint()`
is called by all four clocks and draws only when `indexAt` changes.

Canvas cost, measured in the same engine: **0.0416 ms for a full 480×270 repaint**
(9,041 repaints in 300 ms) — **1.3% of a 30 Hz frame budget**. Drawing one word
is not what will break this; the phone's *scheduler* is.

And the one honest scare: an earlier run dropped exactly **1** word — word 54,
`god`, whose gap to the next is **120 ms** — because the driver had blocked the
main thread for 300 ms to time the canvas. **A 300 ms main-thread stall costs a
word.** That is not a browser fault, it is the shape of the risk: road (a)'s
painter lives on the main thread, and everything else on that thread is its
competitor.

## 4 · K26b and K28 — **the phone's to answer, and the probe is built to answer them**

These cannot be measured off a phone at all, and the 6 Sep probe could not
distinguish "the page froze" from "the compositor stopped". `float.html` counts
**four clocks separately**, in the foreground and while away:

| clock | what it proves if it climbs while backgrounded |
|---|---|
| `raf` | requestAnimationFrame survived (not expected — iOS suspends it when hidden) |
| `timer` | a 60 Hz `setInterval` survived un-throttled (not expected) |
| `timeupdate` | the media element is still firing — ~4 Hz, alive but **17% of words lost** |
| `worklet` | **an `AudioWorkletProcessor` tick on the AUDIO thread** |

The worklet clock is new here and it is the interesting one: iOS keeps the audio
graph running for a backgrounded app that is playing, and in the container it
ticks at **57 Hz** (15,215 ticks in 265 s at one message per 6 quanta). It cannot
draw — drawing is the main thread's — so what it measures is whether the main
thread is still *there to be told*. **`worklet` climbing while `paint` does not
is a live page with a stopped compositor, which is a different and much better
answer than a dead page**, and it is the case `?mode=manual`
(`captureStream(0)` + `track.requestFrame()`) exists to rescue.

`paints while away = 0` **with the PiP window open** is the failure that matters:
a floating box with one frozen word, worse than no float. K28's ten minutes is a
wait, not a press, and `RUN.md` says so in bold because two minutes does not
answer it.

## 5 · K26c — return on the same word: **no new store is needed, and the probe writes the real key**

The float does not need a position of its own. K24 already put an axis position
`v` on the reader's own `wordcursor:<slug>`, and `cursor.js` owns `^wordcursor:`
and nothing else. So `leavepictureinpicture` reads `indexAt(currentTime)` and
writes **that word id** to `wordcursor:<slug>` — the same key `openBook`'s
`seatOnWord` already opens on (13 Sep, G-MEMORY). The probe writes it and prints
it, so the phone press shows the id it left on and the id it came back to, side
by side.

**The float therefore adds no store, no second cursor and no sync surface.**

## 6 · K27 — **no.** One sentence, as asked

**`navigator.mediaSession` cannot carry the ring's questions, because its action
vocabulary is fixed by the spec and has no custom entries** — in headless
Chromium all twelve names (`play` `pause` `stop` `seekbackward` `seekforward`
`seekto` `previoustrack` `nexttrack` `skipad` `togglemicrophone` `togglecamera`
`hangup`) accept a handler **without throwing**, which proves the opposite of
what it looks like: **registration is not capability, only a fired action is** —
and of the ones iOS actually delivers from a headset, `nexttrack` (double-press)
is already `trigger.js`'s and `previoustrack` (triple-press) is already
QUIET.md's `again, slower`, leaving **zero free positions for the four
questions**. There is no hold event in the API at all.

So the lock screen and the headset stay what QUIET.md already decided: transport
plus one repair. **The ring lives on the float's own surface**, which is exactly
why the float has to be able to take a tap.
