# Island test — can the Dynamic Island show the word being spoken?

**Question.** The play-bar drafts (TTSTV `design/playbar/playbar-drafts.html`, commit 0c5d294) put the word being spoken into the Dynamic Island when you leave Frank. Apple limits how often a Live Activity can change and publishes no number, so this measured it on Osca's iPhone (iPhone18,3, iOS 26.6.1), 13 Oct 2026.

## The answer

**Not when the update comes from the app itself.** Once you leave the app, iOS shows about 4 more words in the island and then freezes it. It stays frozen until you open the app again; then it catches up in a burst and freezes again when you leave. The rate made no difference: even one word every 4 seconds froze after 4 words. While the app is open, the island kept up with 4 words a second.

The final run, from the Mac's recording of the phone's screen and the phone's own log, all 4½ minutes:

| seconds after launch | what Osca did | changes seen in the island |
|---|---|---|
| 13 | left the app | — |
| 16–28 | away | 4 words (one every 4 s), then **nothing** |
| 28–64 | away | **0** (the app sent 6 more) |
| 64–73 | opened it twice | 3: caught up |
| 73–193 | away | **0 for 2 minutes** (the app sent 250 more) |
| 193–197 | opened it | 5: caught up, then live at 4 a second while open |
| 197–262 | away | **0** |
| 262–270 | opened it | 15: caught up again |

- **The app was not at fault.** It sent all 310 updates on time and iOS accepted each one in under 15 ms. ActivityKit's own record shows the words held and released in one burst when the app opened.
- **The freeze happens exactly when the drafts need the island**, after you've left Frank.
- **What Osca saw on the phone matched:** it moves, stops, catches up when opened, stops again. The island also grew and shrank with each word until the word was given a fixed-width slot.

Pictures: `island-test/results/island-22s-moving.png` shows a live word. `island-test/results/island-100s-frozen.png` shows the same word still there 80 s later, while the app sent 120 more.

**Since then (runs 4–25, same day):** the island will show words with no updates at all, if the words are drawn by the island's own clocks. A stopwatch the island runs itself redraws 50 times a second for as long as you like; put it in a font whose digits draw a word, and that word appears at the moment you chose, while the app is in the background. Run 17 showed three words landing on the hundredth of a second, each one replacing the last; run 18 showed thirty in a row at 3 a second, every one on time. Runs 19–24 kept dying, and the phone's own log said why: iOS refuses the island's drawing when it is over about 2 MB (`archiveTooLarge`); with the word stack drawn once instead of in every presentation (run 25), 30 words lived for 3 minutes. A background update every 15–30 s arrives within a second (runs 21–22), so a chapter can be fed in windows. Details and what is still unproven are under "Run 16" and "Run 17" below.

What else might work is in `ISLAND-OPTIONS.md`.

## What we made (kept, in `island-test/`)

A small throwaway app, **Island Test**, plus a Mac screen recorder. It's kept apart from Frank: it has its own Xcode project and its own id (`com.ttstv.frank.islandtest`), and nothing in Frank builds it or uses it, so it can't ship with Frank by mistake. Nothing else in either repo was touched.

| file | what it is |
|---|---|
| `App/IslandTest.swift` | The phone side. Plays a quiet looping tone (same background-audio setup as Frank), starts one Live Activity, and when you leave the app sends words at set rates. Logs every update sent, when it was accepted, when ActivityKit says the content changed, and when the app went to the background or came back. |
| `App/AppDelegate.swift` | The app's one screen and its background/foreground hooks. |
| `Widget/IslandWidget.swift` | The Live Activity itself: the island, the long-press view and the lock screen. |
| `Shared/IslandAttributes.swift` | What the app sends the widget, and how a word is laid out (below). |
| `recorder/record.swift` | The Mac side. Records the phone's screen over the cable (the feed QuickTime uses), notes how much the island's strip changed in every frame, and saves a picture of the strip each time it changes. |
| `analyze.py` | Lines the two logs up and prints the table per rate. |
| `run.sh` | The one command: builds both, installs, records, runs, copies the log back, adds the results here and commits them. |
| `project.yml` | The Xcode project, generated by xcodegen. |
| `results/` | The last run: the phone's log, the screen-change log, the summary, the run log and three pictures. |

**How the word is drawn in the island** (Osca, 13 Oct). This is ready to reuse whatever the island ends up doing:
- **Fixed-width slot:** the word sits in a slot of fixed width, so the island doesn't grow and shrink with each word and leaves room for the clock, battery and other indicators.
- **Long words in pieces:** words longer than seven letters are broken into pieces of up to six, for example "acknow-" then "ledged".
- **Font:** Iowan Old Style at 600, warm white `#f3efe6`, red `#ff6a5e`, as the drafts draw it.
- **Red letter:** it follows **Frank's own rule, copied from TTSTV `reader/wordview.js` (`orp` / `pivotIndex`)** so it's the same on every surface. Counting letters only, it's letter 1, 2, 3, 4 or 5 for words of 1, 2–5, 6–9, 10–13 or 14+ letters ("My" → y, "Mr." → r, "He" → e). The red letter always sits in the same column.
- **Not yet seen on the phone:** the font and red-letter rule were fixed after the last run, so Osca hasn't seen them yet.

## How to run it again

From the `IOS TTS TV` folder, with the iPhone plugged in **by cable** and unlocked:

```
bash island-test/run.sh
```

- **Time:** about 7 minutes. Stop it with Ctrl-C if nothing has printed for 14.
- **Camera prompt:** the first time, the Mac may ask whether Terminal may use the Camera. That's the phone's screen feed, so click Allow.
- **On the phone:** when it prints **ON THE PHONE NOW**, swipe up to the home screen and leave the phone alone. Don't tap the island, because that opens the app.

`bash island-test/run.sh` now runs **run 10, the drawing-rules diagnostic** (below); `bash island-test/run.sh words` runs **run 5–9: words only, no clock visible**. Every word is drawn as ordinary text (Frank's island style: Iowan Old Style, red letter by Frank's rule, fixed column), all stacked in one place. Each word sits behind two invisible switches made from the island's own live timers, drawn in the gate font (`font/IslandGate.ttf`, built by `font/build_gate.py`), in which every non-zero digit is a solid block and zeros are nothing. A timer counting up from the word's start is solid only after it; a timer counting down to the word's end is solid only before it. So each word shows only in its own third of a second, and the app sends nothing. 90 words at 3 a second, from 20 s after launch. A red dot left of the camera is a plain check that switches work at all: it should vanish 25 s after launch.

`bash island-test/run.sh clock` runs **run 4, the island's own clock**. The app sends no updates at all; the island shows two clocks drawn in the counting font (`island-test/font/`, built by `font/build_font.py`), in which a clock string such as "0:07" or "0:07.25" is drawn as the one word spoken at that moment, with its red letter on a fixed column. Left of the camera is a stopwatch asking for hundredths; right of it is the whole-second timer. The recorder times each side separately. The test speaks at a steady 3 words a second. The test font is DejaVu Serif, because that is what the build machine had.

`bash island-test/run.sh rates` runs runs 1–3 again: words sent at set rates, slowest first (once every 4 s for 40 s, then 1, 2, 3 and 4 a second for 30 s each, with 10 s of rest between).

## What the island costs the status bar (Osca, 13 Oct 14:57)

Measured on the recordings (402 pt screen; the recorder sees the whole top bar):

- **Pill width with a 14 pt word each side of the camera (4.6 em slots): 265 pt.** The island at the moment of opening is 258 pt; the bare camera cutout about 125 pt; the two slots add about 140 pt.
- **Lost:** the signal bars and Wi-Fi on the right, and the silent-mode bell on the left. **Kept:** the battery, and the clock -- but clipped: the pill starts 36 pt in and "09:41" shows as "9:41".
- **Word pieces:** the splitting rule (`IslandWord.pieces`, pieces of at most 6 letters plus a hyphen) splits "neighbourhood" into three; it should be at most two (7 + 6). The slot could come down from 4.6 em to about 3.8 em at 14 pt before the longest piece is cut.
- **Cover left, word right** (the layout Osca wants once it is stable) should bring the pill to about 225 pt: the clock then fits whole; whether the signal and Wi-Fi come back depends on the right slot's width. To measure: a width ladder (3.0, 3.6, 4.6 em on the right, a 20 pt icon on the left), counting which status icons survive at each step.

## How we got here (three runs, 13 Oct)

1. **Run 1 (10:43): measured nothing.** The widget was meant to report each word it drew back to the app. It never did. Osca saw the words change, then stop.
2. **Run 2 (11:00): measured nothing again.** We gave the widget a shared folder to write in, and it still wrote nothing. A widget's own code can't report what it shows on this phone, so the phone can't measure itself. Osca saw it stop after about 30 s and catch up whenever he opened the app.
3. **Run 3 (11:11): measured.** The Mac recorded the phone's screen over the cable and timed every change, which gave the table above. Runs 1 and 2 are in git history (`b2d7d23`, `ff3ec1b`) if ever needed.

The build folder, the 267 frame pictures and the generated project files from these runs were moved to `_to_delete/island-test-13oct/` for Osca to empty.


### Run 4 (13 Oct 11:59): the island's own clock — what it showed

**The island's clock runs on its own, very fast.** With the app sending nothing at all, the hundredths stopwatch kept counting in the island for the whole 5 minutes ("00:29.92", "00:59.95", "01:00.90", "04:59.95"). The Mac's recording shows it redrawn about **50 times a second**, never pausing more than 0.09 s. The whole-second timer next to it ticked once a second until its 4-minute range ended. So the island isn't the limit: it can change many times a second with no updates.

**But the font's word-swap was not applied.** The island used our font (the digits are its shapes), yet drew the clock one character at a time and showed digits instead of words. That held for both clocks. On a normal computer the same font draws "0:07" as a word. So the island draws clock text glyph by glyph, without the step that joins characters into one drawing.

(The automatic table below reads "2 changes" for the left side. That's wrong. The stopwatch changed so constantly that the analyser's self-set threshold rose above it. With a fixed threshold, the left side changed 49.9 times a second and the right 1.00 time a second.)

**The app went quiet early.** Its log stops 5 s after leaving, and it never wrote its "done" mark, so it was probably suspended or stopped while the island's clocks carried on. Not yet explained.

### Run 8 (13 Oct 13:00), and a correction to runs 5–7

- **What showed:** only the plain white dot. No words, and no check square at 15 s, even though that square was nothing more than a stopwatch drawn in our block font. The island was **not** closed early this time, so plain stopwatches are stable, as in run 4.
- **The cause was my font design, not iOS.** To stack every character of a clock in one spot, I gave every character of the run-5-to-8 fonts **zero width**. Text with no width is simply not drawn. Run 4's font had normal widths and drew perfectly.
- **Correction:** runs 5, 6 and 7 used the same zero-width fonts. So their findings ("a timer mask draws nothing", "a timer drawn over something erases nothing") are **not proven**. Those switches were probably empty for the same reason.
- **Fix (run 9):** only the period has width; every other character has none.
  - In "00:12.34" the two hundredths digits sit after the period. They're the only characters that draw inside the text's own box, and they're non-zero whenever the clock is running.
  - The minutes and seconds digits draw their copy far out of the window.
  - Checked on a normal computer (`island-test/font/window-preview.png`): a running clock shows the black block plus its one word and red letter, and a clock at zero shows nothing.
- **Picture:** `island-test/results/run8-20s-nothing-drawn.png`.

### Run 10 (13 Oct 13:13): all black — the real rule

- **What showed:** nothing at all, not even **A**, which is run 4's own font. That font drew perfectly in run 4.
- **The one difference:** run 4's clock had **already started** when the island drew it. Every clock in runs 5–10 was set to start **in the future**, at its word's moment (or at 15 s for the checks).
- **The rule, almost certainly:** the island never draws a live clock whose start is still in the future, not even once that moment arrives. That's the cause of six blank runs. It's my design again; I should have tested it first.
- **Consequences:**
  - "A clock per word that starts at the word's moment" is impossible.
  - Runs 5–7's findings about masks and erasing are void: those clocks never drew in the first place.
- **Picture:** `island-test/results/run10-26s-all-black.png`.

**Run 11** uses only clocks that have **already started**: a countdown from launch to 25 s, which is the ordinary "arriving in 3:12" kind.
- **Left of the camera:**
  - **A:** run 4's font from launch, which should run.
  - **E:** the same, but starting at 15 s, which should never draw. This confirms the rule.
- **Right of the camera:**
  - **D:** the countdown in the block font. Blocks until 25 s, then nothing.
  - **M:** a white dot masked by the countdown. There until 25 s, then gone.
  - **X:** a pink dot erased by the countdown. Hidden until 25 s, then appears.

If M or X behaves, a running countdown can show and hide things, and the words idea can be rebuilt on countdowns.

### The phone's log (13 Oct 15:16): it is a size cap, not memory

`island-test/phone-log.sh` pulled the phone's own log. Every dismissal is the same line from chronod, the process that runs widgets:

    reload: failed during processing ... ChronoCore.RequestCompletionOperation.Error.archiveTooLarge(fileSize: 2036440 B)   (run 24, 30 layers)
    ... archiveTooLarge(fileSize: 3858104 B)   (run 23, 60 layers)

Every ~15 s (and on every update) iOS asks the widget to re-render; the result is an archive of the drawn views, with the clock glyphs pre-rendered so the island can tick them by itself. Ours is about **65 KB per word layer**; an archive over about 2 MB is refused, the reload fails ("next attempt due" in ten minutes), and the activity is torn down. That is every "dismissed" from run 19 to run 24, and why the moment varied: it is whichever reload came first. 300 layers was 20 MB, refused at the first reload. Memory never came into it, and the updates never did either.

Why 65 KB a layer: the archive holds all four presentations (lock screen, expanded, compact, minimal) and the stack was in every one, so each layer was drawn four times at four sizes. Run 25 keeps the stack only in the compact slot (the other three show a label), which should cut the archive about four times; the black cover box in each glyph (a wide rectangle rendered per digit) can shrink to the word's own bounds for more. The log is `island-test/results/phone-log.txt`; the archive `results/phone-1516.logarchive` is not committed.

### What a word costs the island, and run 27 (Osca, 15:43: "optimise")

The island cannot run our font; at each render it pre-draws a picture of every glyph a clock view might show (0–9, colon, dot) so it can swap pictures by itself 50 times a second. So one word layer cost ten pictures, each of them the word **plus a black cover 4.6 em wide**, at 3x pixels -- and until run 25, in four presentations. Three cuts, the mechanism untouched:

1. Four presentations → one (run 25, done): the 30 pt lock-screen copy alone was ~5x the compact one.
2. Nine identical digit glyphs → **one shared glyph** for digits 1–9, stored once instead of nine times.
3. The cover box → **the union of this word's and the previous word's bounds** (the previous word is known when the font is built), instead of a 4.6 em slab.

### The phone's log for run 28 (16:05): it is a permission, not a budget

    15:55:02  liveactivitiesd: Process is doing more than playing background media so is permitted to update activity   (update 1, shown)
    15:55:12  liveactivitiesd: Process is doing more than playing background media so is permitted to update activity   (update 2, shown)
    15:55:12.612  runningboardd: Assertion did invalidate due to timeout: "Activity change delivery" (granted at 15:54:42 when the activity started; 30 s)
    15:55:22  liveactivitiesd: Client is not permitted to update activity   (update 3)
    15:55:32  liveactivitiesd: Client is not permitted to update activity   (update 4)

**An app whose only reason for running in the background is audio is not allowed to update its Live Activity.** It is allowed while it holds any other background reason. iOS lends one for free when the activity starts -- a 30-second "activity change delivery" assertion -- which is why day 1's runs showed about four words and froze, why run 22 got three updates 15 s apart, and why run 28 got two 10 s apart and lost the third 22 ms after that assertion expired. The budget itself is not the limit: chronod marks each local update "overridden for budget exempt reason: free".

So the fix is a second background reason held for the whole read. Run 29 is run 28 plus one line in the app: a UIKit background task begun at launch and never ended (while audio plays, iOS reports unlimited background time, so it does not expire). Nothing changes in the widget.

## The decision (end of 13 Oct)

**The island works as Frank's phone float in every respect but one.** Proven on the phone, with the app in the background: one word at a time in Frank's red-letter style, fixed slot, 3 a second, landing on the hundredth; windows of 30 words, two alive, 90 words running continuously across the joins; under the island's drawing cap (~2 MB) and the widget's memory limit (60 MB), both named in the phone's log.

**The one thing it cannot do: the app may not re-arm the island from the background.** An app running in the background only for audio is not permitted to update its Live Activity; iOS lends 30 seconds when the activity starts and that is all (liveactivitiesd, run 28's log). Nothing the app can hold without a user-facing permission changes that.

**The route Apple permits is push.** A relay (the Modal endpoint already in Frank's architecture is the natural place, with an APNs key) sends each window's content through APNs; the island updates with the app asleep. The content per push is tiny (two dates). This is a product decision -- it puts a server in the float's path -- and it is Osca's to make. If it is made, the remaining work is the three steps already listed: windows by push, fonts for a chapter at runtime, the design passes.
